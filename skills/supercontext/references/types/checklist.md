# Type: checklist

Criteria the assistant checks work against before delivering or approving it: a publishing checklist, a contract review, a campaign QA, an accessibility check. It is consulted at the end of a task, or when reviewing someone else's work.

## What makes it usable
- When the checklist is used, and on what work.
- Criteria that can be checked by looking at the work, each with what passes and what fails.
- What to do when a criterion fails: fix it, flag it, or stop and escalate.
- Which criteria block delivery and which are only recommendations.

## Diagnosis questions
- On which work, and at which moment, is the check run?
- What do reviewers send back most often today?
- Which failures block delivery, and which only get a note?
- Are there criteria that depend on the channel, client or type of piece?
- What should the review return: a corrected version, a list of issues, or a pass or fail?

## Materials that help
An existing checklist, review comments on real work, a rejected piece with the reasons, and the rules or guidelines the checklist enforces.

## Typical skeleton
1. When to use it, and what the review returns
2. Blocking criteria
3. Recommended criteria
4. Criteria by channel or type, when they differ
5. What to do when something fails

## Rules for this type
- Each criterion is a yes or no question someone can answer by looking at the work: "Does every price show the currency and two decimals?", not "Are prices correct?".
- Put the fix or action next to the criterion when it isn't obvious ("If not, rewrite with the currency symbol").
- Order criteria as the review goes: what to check first, or what most often fails, comes first.
- Mark which criteria block delivery; a long list of equal criteria makes the model treat a typo like a legal error.
- If a criterion depends on a rule detailed in another file, name that rule's file and section exactly instead of copying the rule.
- Keep the list as short as the use cases allow; criteria nobody fails are noise.

## Pitfalls
- Vague criteria ("good quality", "on brand") that each reviewer reads differently.
- Criteria that restate whole guidelines instead of checking them.
- No instruction on what to do when a check fails.
- Mixing the checklist with the procedure to produce the work.

## Sample entry for the plan
Two criteria as they will appear, one blocking and one recommended, each with its action on failure.
