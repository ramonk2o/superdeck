# Hook, memorable moment and close

Used in step 1.4 and in the express proposal.

## Hook
How the first 30 seconds — or the first slide, in a reading deck — create a tension that only the central idea resolves.
- Types: a number that contradicts what the audience believes; the question they already bring into the room; a concrete case (a customer, a situation); the cost of not acting; a provocation.
- The hook has to lead to the central idea; a loose trick grabs attention and then wastes it.

## Memorable moment
The one thing the audience will remember and repeat the next day, placed at the turning point of the arc.
- Types: a figure revealed dramatically (a big number alone, a chart whose reading changes); an unexpected comparison; a real story; a demonstration; a metaphor that becomes an image (Phase 2 gives it form).
- Test: would someone tell a colleague about it in the hallway?

## Close
The last slide is the ask or the concrete action — who does what, by when — and, when possible, it returns to the hook to close the circle. "Thank you" or "Questions?" is never the last content slide.
