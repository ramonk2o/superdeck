# Case: project status

Follow-up decks: committees, weekly or monthly reports, check-ins with a client. The audience wants to know **whether everything is fine, what is worrying, and what is needed from them**.

## What the audience needs to leave knowing
- The overall status, at a glance.
- The risks and blockers that matter.
- The decisions that depend on them.

## Diagnosis questions
- What is the overall status compared with the plan: on time, at risk, late?
- What has moved forward since last time that matters to this audience?
- Which risks or blockers exist, and what is the impact of each?
- Which decisions or support do you need to leave the meeting with?
- What changed in scope, schedule or budget?
- What does this audience already know (so you don't repeat it)?

## How to phrase the central idea
Status + what needs attention.
- Weak: "Update on project X."
- Strong: "The launch is still set for December, but the partner integration slipped 2 weeks — we need to decide today whether to cut the cashback module from the MVP."

## Recommended arcs
- **Status first** (default): traffic light → what needs a decision → details.
- **By workstream**, when the audience follows specific workstreams.

## Typical skeleton
1. Overall status + decisions needed
2. What moved forward (only what is relevant)
3. Risks and blockers, with impact and plan
4. Decisions needed, with options and a recommendation
5. Next steps until the next meeting
Appendix: detailed schedule, status by task.

## Pitfalls
- A list of activities instead of status ("we had a meeting, sent an email…").
- Hiding or softening delays until they become a crisis.
- Leaving the decision request for the last slide, when there's no time left.
- Repeating what the audience already knows.
- An all-green traffic light that doesn't reflect reality.

## Typical visuals
Traffic light/status indicators, timeline with milestones and progress, risk table (impact × probability), decision table with options.

## Specific stress test
- If the meeting ends in 5 minutes, which decision must have been made?
- Is there a risk you think the audience doesn't know about? Why isn't it on the first slide?
- Should any green here be yellow?
