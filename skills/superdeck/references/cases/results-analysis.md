# Case: results analysis

Decks that show how something performed: a campaign, a CRM program, a channel, a product, a period. The audience wants to know three things, in this order: **did it work? why? what do we do now?**

## What the audience needs to leave knowing
- The verdict, in one sentence, compared with a reference (target, previous period, control group, benchmark).
- The few factors that explain the result.
- What changes from here: keep, adjust, scale or stop.

## Diagnosis questions
- What was expected? Is there a target, a previous period or a control group to compare with?
- Who will decide what with this result?
- Which metric actually matters to this audience (revenue, conversion, retention…) and which are only intermediate (opens, clicks)?
- Does the result have a causal explanation or only a correlation? Were there external factors (seasonality, a change in the base, another campaign running at the same time)?
- Is the sample or volume large enough to claim anything?
- Did something not work? How does it come in?
- What is the recommendation?

## How to phrase the central idea
Verdict + cause + implication.
- Weak: "The October campaign had good results."
- Strong: "The reactivation program brought back 8% of inactive customers, driven by the personalized-offer push — it is worth scaling to the whole base."

## Recommended arcs
- **Answer first** (default): verdict → why → what to do. Best for managers and decision-makers.
- **SCR**, when the result contradicts what the audience expects.
- **Before / after / bridge**, when there was a clear change of approach.

## Typical skeleton
1. Verdict + recommendation
2. The number that matters vs. the reference
3–5. The factors that explain it (one per slide)
6. What didn't work and what we learned
7. Next steps with owners and deadlines
Appendix: methodology, full metrics, segmentations.

## Pitfalls
- Following the dashboard's order (every metric, one after another) instead of the story's order.
- Vanity metrics in the spotlight (opens, impressions) when the audience wants business results.
- A number without comparison: "12% conversion" doesn't say whether it is good or bad.
- Hiding or diluting what went wrong — it takes down the credibility of the rest.
- Explaining the what without the why.
- Treating correlation as cause.
- Decimal places and precision nobody needs.

## Typical visuals
Bar vs. target, line with annotated events, waterfall to break down the change, conversion funnel, small multiples to compare segments, big number with context.

## Specific stress test
- If the director reads only the first slide, do they know whether it worked and what to do?
- Would this result have happened anyway? How do you know?
- Which metric would a skeptic ask for first, and is it in the deck?
