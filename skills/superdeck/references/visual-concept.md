# Visual concept

Used in step 2.1 and in the express proposal.

## Before proposing
Know what constrains the concept: start from the identity materials gathered in the opening. If none came and the session can list design systems saved in the user's account, list them. If you still don't know whether there is a mandatory template or brand manual, ask in the same round in which you propose the concepts.

## Proposing
Propose 2 or 3 concepts derived from the central idea, the arc, the memorable moment, the audience and the tone. For each:
- a short name;
- the concept in one sentence;
- how it shows up in the deck (cover, charts, transitions, memorable moment);
- its palette and type direction, in one line;
- why it serves the message;
- its risk or limit.

Recommend one.

## Examples
To calibrate, not to copy:
- "Everything told with simple geometric shapes only — the data becomes shape."
- "Each finding is an infographic that explains itself."
- "The whole deck revolves around the image of an iceberg: on top, the metrics everyone sees; below, the revenue nobody looks at."
- "Control-panel language."
- "Full-screen documentary photography of the real customer."
- "A route map, in which each section is a stop on the way to the destination."

## Test before recommending
- Does the concept reinforce the central idea, or is it just decoration?
- Does it work on the hardest slide, the one with the densest data?
- Does it fit the brand identity and the mandatory template?
- Can it be executed with the build options this session offers?

For a sober reading deck, a discreet concept (a system of colors, shapes and grid) usually works better than a strong metaphor.
