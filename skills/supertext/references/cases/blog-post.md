# Case: blog post

A post for a blog or newsletter, read by someone who chose to click and can leave at any line. The reader wants to know **quickly whether it is worth reading, and to leave with something they can use or repeat**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- From the title and the first paragraph: whether the post is for them.
- One idea or technique they can use or repeat.
- Proof that it's real: what happened, with the author's specifics.

## Materials that help
The notes or thread the post comes from; numbers, screenshots, links; a past post or newsletter issue the author liked; the outlet's guidelines, if any.

## Diagnosis questions
- Who exactly is this for, and what are they trying to do when they find it?
- What should they be able to do or decide after reading?
- What is the one example, number or story of yours that proves it?
- Where does it run (company blog, personal newsletter), and is there a length or voice guide?
- Is there a call to action? If the post's main job is sign-ups or sales, it is copy and out of scope.

## How to phrase the central idea
Takeaway + proof. The position is the author's, as "Propose before asking" in `SKILL.md` says.
- Weak: "Some lessons from our database migration."
- Strong: "Our cutover took three weekends instead of one because we tested without production traffic; test under real load before you book the date."

## Typical structures
- **Lesson learned:** what we tried → what happened → why → what to do instead.
- **How-to:** the result first → the steps → the pitfalls → when not to do this.
- **Take:** the claim → the case behind it → the objection → what to do. If it argues like an essay, it is a hybrid, as 0.3 in `SKILL.md` says.
- **Newsletter issue:** one main item in one of the forms above, then short items.
- Headings only past about 800 words.

## Pitfalls
- A label title, or a title that promises more than the post delivers.
- A preamble before the point; the reader leaves by line three.
- Generic advice anyone could write, when the value is what only the author saw.
- A call to action that takes over.
- Bullet lists in place of the story of what happened.

## Sample passage for the plan
The title and the first paragraph, where the reader decides to stay.
