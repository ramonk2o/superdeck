# Case: book chapter

A chapter of a nonfiction book, read as part of a whole. The reader wants **to understand where they are in the book and to have a reason to turn the page**.

## What the reader needs to leave with
- Where the chapter sits in the book's argument: what it builds on and what it adds.
- The chapter's one claim or question, with enough story or evidence to hold it.
- A reason to turn the page: an open question the next chapter takes up.

## Materials that help
The book's outline; the previous chapter or its summary; the chapter's notes and sources, with pages; the style sheet approved for an earlier chapter; the publisher's length and citation rules.

## Diagnosis questions
- What is the book's central argument, and what does this chapter add to it?
- What did the previous chapter establish, and what does the next one need from this one?
- How much does the reader know by this point?
- What carries the chapter: a story, a case, research, an argument?
- Which sources must be cited, and in what style?
- What length, and which of the book's conventions apply (epigraphs, section breaks, summaries)?

## How to phrase the central idea
The chapter's claim + its role in the book.
- Weak: "This chapter covers the history of credit cards."
- Strong: "Credit cards spread because merchants needed them first; that origin explains why fees still land on merchants, which chapter 5 takes up."

## Typical structures
- **Narrative-led:** an opening scene → the question it raises → three to five sections alternating story and explanation → back to the scene → a bridge to the next chapter.
- **Argument-led:** the claim and why it matters at this point of the book → evidence sections → the objection → what follows → a bridge.
- **Chronological:** periods as sections, each stating its point, not just its dates.

## Pitfalls
- Re-explaining what earlier chapters established, or assuming what they didn't.
- A chapter that reads as a standalone essay, or one that only sets up the next.
- Voice drifting from earlier chapters: reuse their approved style sheet.
- Long quotes as filler.
- Reconstructed scenes or dialogue with details no source gives.

## Sample passage for the plan
The opening page, which sets the chapter's voice. If the book's style was already approved for an earlier chapter, the hardest passage instead, usually the one that explains the core concept.

At delivery, offer the style sheet and a three-line summary of the chapter, to bring when planning the next one.
