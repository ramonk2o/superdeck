# Writing the text

Used in step 2.1. These are rules the model doesn't follow on its own; they hold for every kind of text, and the style sheet overrides them when it asks for something else. Facts follow "Nothing invented" in `SKILL.md`.

## Opening
- The first lines do the job the plan gave them. No warm-up, no restating the request, no "in this text I will".

## Paragraphs and claims
- One claim per paragraph, with its evidence or example next to it.
- Every abstract claim gets a specific detail from the plan: a number, a case, a name, something the author saw.
- Certainty matches the evidence: state what is supported, mark what is inferred, and don't use precise numbers you can't back.
- One term per concept; don't vary words for elegance when the reader could take them for different things.

## Habits to avoid
These mark a text as written on autopilot:
- "Not X, but Y" reversals.
- Lists of three by reflex.
- A rhetorical question as a transition.
- An ending that restates the text, or "in conclusion".
- Stacked hedges and intensifiers.
- Frequent dashes.
- Bullet lists where the text should argue.

## Format follows the destination
- **Email:** plain text, no headings, the ask in the first lines, short paragraphs.
- **Blog post:** headings only when the post is long.
- **Decision record:** the record's usual headings (context, options, decision, consequences), unless the plan says otherwise.
- **Citations:** as the Text plan says.

## Length
Stay within about 10% of the length in the plan. If the content needs more, ask.
